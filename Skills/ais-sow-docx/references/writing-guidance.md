# AIS SOW Writing Guidance

Use this standard for all AIS-authored, client-visible Statement of Work (SOW)
content. It governs authored narrative, not fixed legal clauses. Approved legal
and commercial language takes precedence and must not be rewritten without the
appropriate review.

## Audience

Write one document that can be understood by:

- client executives evaluating the intended value and delivery boundaries;
- client procurement, contracts, and legal reviewers evaluating commitments;
- client and AIS delivery leads planning responsibilities and decisions; and
- technical reviewers validating feasibility, dependencies, and evidence.

Assume that not every reader knows AIS methods, the source proposal, or the
technical domain. Include the context needed to interpret the agreement, but do
not teach general technology or repeat background that does not affect scope.

## AIS Posture

The SOW should present AIS as:

- **Client-centered**: connect the work to the client's stated priorities and
  operating context.
- **Outcome-oriented**: describe the result the services are intended to
  support, then define the deliverables and evidence AIS controls.
- **Collaborative**: make mutual responsibilities and decisions explicit
  without assigning blame.
- **Accountable**: state what AIS will do, what AIS will deliver, and how the
  work will be reviewed.
- **Technically credible**: use precise terms and realistic boundaries rather
  than hype.
- **Commercially disciplined**: avoid hidden scope, unsupported promises, and
  obligations that depend on unconfirmed assumptions.

This posture is confident without being promotional, precise without being
adversarial, and professional without sounding mechanical or legalistic.

## Voice and Tone

- Lead with the decision, result, deliverable, or commitment that matters.
- Use direct, factual, and calm language.
- Prefer short paragraphs and sentences that contain one main obligation.
- Use plain words when they preserve the exact meaning.
- Be specific about scope and acceptance without overselling the work.
- State constraints and risks objectively. Do not dramatize, minimize, or
  assign fault.
- Do not use promotional superlatives such as *best-in-class*, *world-class*,
  *game-changing*, *revolutionary*, or *cutting-edge*.
- Do not use exclamation points, rhetorical questions, conversational asides,
  or informal drafting commentary.

## Sentence and Grammar Standard

- Prefer active voice: `AIS will facilitate two design workshops.`
- Name the actor. Do not write `Workshops will be conducted` when the
  responsible party matters.
- Use present tense for facts and document mechanics: `This SOW defines...`.
- Use `will` for a bounded obligation: `AIS will deliver...` or `The Client
  will provide...`.
- Use conditional language only when the condition is explicit: `If access is
  not available by Milestone 1, the parties will assess the schedule impact
  through change control.`
- Avoid `shall` in authored narrative unless Contracts requires it or it is
  part of approved fixed language.
- Do not use contractions in authored SOW content.
- Use one consistent term for each concept. Do not alternate among *solution*,
  *platform*, *system*, and *environment* unless they identify different things.
- Define an acronym on first use unless the approved template or governing
  agreement already defines it. Avoid acronyms that serve only AIS internal
  processes.
- Avoid `etc.`, `and so on`, `as needed`, `where appropriate`, `timely`,
  `reasonable`, and similar catch-all phrases unless a measurable boundary
  follows.
- Remove unnecessary adverbs and modifiers such as *very*, *highly*,
  *seamlessly*, *quickly*, and *effectively* when they add no testable meaning.
- Use parallel construction in lists and tables. Start comparable items with
  comparable verbs or nouns.
- Use the serial comma. Use sentence case for narrative headings unless the
  approved template controls heading style.
- Write dates, quantities, roles, and named artifacts consistently throughout
  the document.

A useful commitment sentence usually contains:

```text
defined party + will + concrete action + bounded object + timing/evidence when relevant
```

Example: `AIS will configure the approved reporting workspace and demonstrate
the three acceptance scenarios during the solution handoff.`

## Defined Parties

- Use `AIS`, `Client`, `the Client`, `Microsoft`, or another defined party name.
  Match the defined form in the approved agreement. Do not use `we`, `us`,
  `you`, or `your` in obligations.
- Match the party term in the approved agreement. Use `Customer` rather than
  `the Client` when the ECIF or governing form defines that term.
- Do not speak for a third party. State a third-party action as a dependency or
  responsibility only when the source supports it.
- Assign one accountable party to each obligation. Use `the parties` only for
  a genuinely mutual action, such as approving a change.
- Keep AIS responsibilities and client responsibilities balanced and located
  in the corresponding sections.

## Commitments and Outcomes

Use `AIS will` only for services, actions, or deliverables that are in scope,
source-supported, and substantially within AIS control.

AIS may commit to actions such as:

- facilitate, assess, configure, develop, document, demonstrate, validate,
  review, coordinate, provide, deliver, or support a named activity; and
- submit a named artifact or perform a named verification by an agreed
  milestone.

Do not guarantee business adoption, savings, revenue, compliance or
certification, security, availability, performance, funding, third-party
behavior, approvals, or a schedule independent of stated dependencies.

When AIS does not fully control the result, write that the services `support`,
`enable`, `inform`, or `are intended to` advance the outcome.

| Use | Avoid |
|-----|-------|
| `The roadmap is intended to support the Client's modernization decisions.` | `The roadmap will ensure modernization success.` |
| `AIS will validate the configuration against the agreed scenarios.` | `AIS guarantees the solution will meet every future need.` |
| `AIS will provide support for the named components during the coverage window.` | `AIS will provide unlimited support.` |

Do not invent warranties, service levels, remedies, indemnities, acceptance
periods, review cycles, or commercial terms. Preserve approved terms and route
new legal or commercial language to the appropriate reviewer.

## Activities, Deliverables, Milestones, and Acceptance

Keep these concepts distinct:

- **Activity**: work AIS performs, such as a workshop, assessment, build, or
  validation session.
- **Deliverable**: a tangible or inspectable output submitted to the named
  recipient, such as a design package, configured environment, report, or
  demonstration.
- **Milestone**: a meaningful delivery state or decision point, not merely a
  calendar label.
- **Outcome**: a benefit or changed condition the work is intended to support.
- **Acceptance criterion**: observable evidence that the named deliverable
  satisfies the agreed scope.

For each deliverable:

1. Name the artifact or inspectable result.
2. Describe its bounded contents.
3. State when or at which milestone it is submitted, if source-supported.
4. Define observable acceptance evidence.
5. Identify the reviewer or approval mechanism when known.

Acceptance criteria should be specific, measurable in context, traceable to the
deliverable, and limited to the agreed scope. Prefer demonstration results,
required sections, approved scenarios, recorded decisions, or other inspectable
evidence. Avoid `to the Client's satisfaction`, `complete`, `production-ready`,
or `industry standard` unless the governing agreement defines the measure.

An activity is not a deliverable. Attendance is not acceptance unless the
engagement is explicitly a workshop service and the approved proof is the
session record.

## Responsibilities and Dependencies

Write each responsibility as an action assigned to one defined party. Include
the provision, needed date or milestone, and objective impact when relevant.

| Use | Avoid |
|-----|-------|
| `The Client will provide read access to the named data source before the discovery milestone.` | `Client provides data.` |
| `If the access is not available by that milestone, the parties will assess scope and schedule impacts through change control.` | `Client delays will cause the project to fail.` |

- Describe dependencies as facts, not accusations.
- Do not characterize a delay as a client failure, fault, inability, or
  refusal.
- When timing is unknown, tie the responsibility to a named milestone or mark
  the date as unresolved. Do not invent a date.
- State a consequence only when it is supported and proportionate. Prefer
  review through the agreed change process over unilateral remedies.
- Do not hide an obligation inside an assumption.
- Do not use an assumption to transfer an uncontrolled risk without making the
  dependency and response explicit.

## Risks, Exclusions, and Change Control

- State a risk as a possible condition, its delivery impact, the response, and
  the responsible owner when known.
- Use neutral likelihood and impact language. Avoid alarmist phrasing.
- Write exclusions clearly enough to prevent a reasonable adjacent-scope
  assumption. Do not use exclusions to disclaim obligations already stated
  elsewhere.
- Distinguish a current exclusion from a possible future phase.
- Describe change control as a predictable mutual process: written request,
  impact assessment, decision, and the appropriate amendment or change order.
- Do not promise an impact-assessment turnaround unless a source or approved
  policy establishes it.
- Do not use blame, threats, or informal negotiation history to justify a
  change-control rule.

## Terminology

- Use the client's established names for business capabilities, teams, and
  systems when those names are source-supported.
- Use official product and service names. Define technical shorthand on first
  use.
- Distinguish environments, data domains, interfaces, and delivery phases
  consistently.
- Use `client` as a descriptive word and the agreement's defined party term
  (`the Client` or `Customer`) as the party name.
- Use `AIS will` for obligations, an imperative verb for activities in tables,
  and a noun phrase for deliverable names.
- Do not expose internal skill names, agent terminology, repository structure,
  quality-gate labels, or source-trace identifiers as client terminology.

## Client-Content Boundary

The canonical Markdown SOW may contain AIS-only planning and delivery controls.
The client DOCX may contain only approved administrative fields, fixed legal
content, and client-visible narrative.

Keep the following outside client-visible prose:

- slash commands and internal workflow names;
- repository, branch, pull-request, spec-directory, or local file paths;
- source IDs, traceability bookmarks, tier labels, and source-authority notes;
- QA/QC labels, readiness checklists, evidence status, and test or rendering
  results;
- green-sheet names, internal allocation worksheets, profitability review, and
  internal approval-chain commentary;
- prompts, model or agent instructions, drafting notes, TODO markers, and
  placeholder instructions;
- negotiation history, staffing concerns, procurement frustrations, and
  internal risk commentary; and
- research sources or examples from other clients.

If the client contract requires the underlying information, rewrite it in
client terms. For example, state the agreed governance process rather than the
internal command that AIS uses to manage it. Keep machine traceability in
bookmarks and evidence artifacts rather than visible narrative.

`TBD - Commercial Review` is the controlled exception for commercial value
cells in generated drafts. A brief note may state that the values require AIS
commercial review; do not expose internal pricing, profitability, negotiation,
or approval-chain commentary.

## Deterministic Screening

The generator blocks these high-confidence categories before publication:

| Code | What it detects |
|------|-----------------|
| `INTERNAL_WORKFLOW` | AIS commands, paths, source labels, and workflow terminology |
| `DRAFTING_MARKER` | TODOs, internal-use labels, and insert-text placeholders |
| `PROMOTIONAL_CLAIM` | Contract-inappropriate superlatives and hype |
| `UNSUPPORTED_GUARANTEE` | Explicit guarantees of uncontrolled results |
| `OPEN_ENDED_COMMITMENT` | Unlimited, catch-all, or unbounded service promises |
| `BLAME_LANGUAGE` | Client-fault or client-failure wording |
| `CONTRACTION` | Informal contractions in authored narrative |
| `VAGUE_CATCH_ALL` | `etc.` and `and so on` |

The screen is intentionally narrow. A passing result does not prove factual
accuracy, grammar, tone, contractual sufficiency, legal approval, commercial
approval, or client acceptance. Never suppress a finding merely to obtain a
passing result; revise the source or have a qualified reviewer determine
whether the underlying information belongs in client-visible language.

## Pre-Delivery Content Review

A qualified human reviewer must inspect the generated client DOCX and confirm:

- [ ] The opening states the client context and intended outcome without hype.
- [ ] Each obligation names the responsible defined party.
- [ ] Every `AIS will` statement is in scope, source-supported, bounded, and
      substantially within AIS control.
- [ ] Intended benefits are not written as guaranteed results.
- [ ] Activities, deliverables, milestones, and outcomes are not conflated.
- [ ] Each deliverable has observable acceptance evidence tied to its scope.
- [ ] Client and third-party responsibilities state the needed provision and
      timing or milestone when known.
- [ ] Dependency impacts are objective and route through the agreed process
      without blame.
- [ ] Assumptions, exclusions, risks, and change rules are consistent with the
      scope and do not create hidden obligations.
- [ ] Defined parties, product names, acronyms, dates, and key terms are used
      consistently.
- [ ] The prose is concise, active, grammatical, and free of vague catch-all
      language.
- [ ] No AIS-only workflow, source, drafting, research, approval, or evidence
      context appears in client-visible content.
- [ ] Fixed approved clauses are unchanged and any new legal or commercial
      language has the appropriate review.
- [ ] Scope, milestones, responsibilities, and acceptance do not contradict
      another section or table.

Record the reviewer, pass/fail result, and concise notes in the SOW evidence.
This review is a content-readiness control, not a substitute for Contracts,
commercial, legal, or client approval.
